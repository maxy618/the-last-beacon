# THE LAST BEACON

## 1. Research the original project

Thoroughly investigate and reverse-engineer the entire repository:

https://github.com/Noniv/abyssal-garden

Do not limit yourself to reading its README. Study the complete source code, every module, shader, mathematical algorithm, rendering pipeline, build system, and implementation detail.

Understand how the original project achieves its visual quality without external assets or a traditional game engine.

Pay particular attention to its procedural geometry generation, mathematical terrain representation, noise functions, Surface Nets meshing, WebGPU compute shaders, WGSL rendering, instanced geometry, spatial culling, level of detail, lighting, shadows, volumetric effects, post-processing, GPU memory management, dynamic resolution, performance measurement, and procedural audio.

Run the original game and explore it yourself. Capture screenshots, inspect different viewpoints, play with its settings, and understand its actual visual quality and performance.

Treat the repository as an engineering reference. Learn from its techniques, improve them, combine them with other approaches, and develop something substantially more ambitious.

## 2. Game concept

Create a complete, exceptionally beautiful first-person exploration adventure called **THE LAST BEACON**.

The setting is a mysterious, remote island surrounded by a vast ocean. It contains dense forests, dramatic coastlines, mountains, cliffs, caves, rivers, waterfalls, and numerous abandoned human structures.

The island must be procedurally generated. Every new game should create a genuinely different, coherent and believable world.

The lighthouse is the central landmark and one of the most important locations. Its exterior, architecture, proportions, details, interior layout, and surrounding environment must also be procedurally generated. Every world should have its own unique lighthouse.

The island must contain a substantial variety of procedurally generated buildings and structures: abandoned houses, towers, industrial facilities, research stations, ruins, underground spaces, and other interesting locations.

Buildings must be fully explorable. The player must be able to enter them, explore rooms, walk through corridors, climb staircases, open doors, discover hidden areas, and interact with their contents.

Generate convincing architectural structures rather than random collections of geometric shapes. Interiors must correspond correctly to their exteriors, with believable layouts, proportions, connections, and structural details.

The world should feel handcrafted even though its geometry and architecture are generated procedurally.

## 3. No pre-made game assets

A fundamental requirement inspired directly by Abyssal Garden:

**Do not use external artistic assets.**

Do not download or import pre-made 3D models, textures, images, vegetation models, material packs, audio samples, music recordings, HDRIs, skyboxes, or asset packs.

Generate the entire visual world programmatically.

This includes terrain, rocks, trees, plants, buildings, architectural details, furniture, props, materials, surface details, environmental effects, animations, and all other visible content.

Create geometry mathematically, use procedural mesh generation, generate materials and texture details through shaders or other programmatic techniques, and synthesize sound in real time.

Third-party programming libraries and development tools are allowed. There are no restrictions on frameworks or code dependencies. The restriction concerns pre-made artistic assets, not reusable software libraries.

Do not replace missing assets with generic placeholder geometry. Build convincing, detailed procedural alternatives.

The final game should have no dependency on external artistic asset files.

## 4. Story and narrative

Create a genuinely compelling, carefully written story.

The narrative should feel as though it was written by a talented human narrative designer, not generated by an AI.

Avoid generic AI-style dialogue, repetitive exposition, unnatural conversations, predictable dramatic phrases, excessive philosophical monologues, and artificial emotional writing.

Characters, documents, environmental details, and discoveries should have believable personalities, context, and purpose.

Tell the story through exploration, environmental storytelling, discoveries, interactions, and natural dialogue.

Create meaningful quests and objectives that are organically connected to the island and its history.

The player should always have a reasonable understanding of what they are trying to accomplish. Display the current objective in a persistent, unobtrusive, beautifully integrated journal or quest interface that the player can easily refer to.

However, do not rely on conventional quest markers, giant directional arrows, glowing trails, minimap icons, or constant navigation indicators.

Guide the player naturally through visual landmarks, environmental composition, architecture, subtle clues, interesting sounds, documents, dialogue, and contextual hints.

The player should be able to figure out where to go through observation and understanding rather than blindly following markers.

Hints must be subtle but fair. Avoid both excessive hand-holding and frustratingly obscure progression.

Make exploration rewarding. Discoveries should reveal something interesting, change the player's understanding of the island, or provide meaningful gameplay opportunities.

## 5. Gameplay and interaction

Use a first-person perspective with intuitive FPS-style controls.

Create polished movement, responsive camera control, satisfying interactions, and a coherent gameplay loop.

The player should be able to explore naturally, inspect objects, collect items, operate mechanisms, investigate buildings, discover hidden spaces, and solve environmental and mechanical puzzles.

Develop meaningful progression and a satisfying overarching objective involving the lighthouse.

Make the actual experience enjoyable, not merely visually impressive.

Pay attention to game feel, movement responsiveness, interaction feedback, navigation, puzzle design, and the pacing of exploration.

## 6. Geometry, physics and collision

Collision and interaction must be implemented throughout the entire world.

Visible geometry and collision geometry must correspond correctly. Walls, floors, ceilings, staircases, doorways, furniture, rocks, buildings, terrain, and other relevant objects must behave appropriately.

Prevent clipping, floating, walking through solid surfaces, incorrect stair navigation, broken doorways, and inaccessible interiors.

Ensure that procedurally generated buildings are actually navigable and that their interiors remain connected correctly to their exteriors.

Interactions must work reliably and feel natural.

Test collisions and navigation in many independently generated worlds, not just one manually inspected location.

## 7. Visual quality

**Visual quality is the absolute priority.**

The final game should substantially surpass Abyssal Garden in graphical fidelity, environmental detail, rendering sophistication, atmosphere, geometry quality, and overall visual richness.

Do not settle for merely matching the original.

Create exceptionally detailed environments with convincing geometry, sophisticated materials, realistic vegetation, intricate architecture, high-quality lighting, shadows, reflections, water, atmospheric scattering, volumetric effects, weather, particles, and environmental animation.

Pay particular attention to details that make environments feel physically believable.

Interiors must receive the same level of visual attention as exterior environments.

Avoid obvious procedural repetition, simplistic geometry, generic materials, artificial-looking lighting, and empty environments.

The objective is to make the player genuinely impressed when looking around, entering a building, discovering a hidden location, or approaching the lighthouse.

## 8. Performance and optimization

Visual quality must not come at the expense of performance.

Aim for exceptional graphical fidelity while maintaining genuinely smooth, stable gameplay.

Optimize GPU utilization, CPU overhead, memory usage, geometry processing, rendering, shadows, lighting, procedural generation, collision detection, and all other relevant systems.

Study the optimization techniques used by Abyssal Garden and independently discover better approaches where possible.

Implement appropriate level of detail, culling, instancing, efficient geometry management, adaptive rendering quality, and other optimizations as justified by profiling.

Avoid unnecessary per-frame allocations, excessive draw calls, redundant calculations, and avoidable synchronization bottlenecks.

Implement scalable graphics settings without making lower-quality configurations look fundamentally broken.

Measure actual performance. Track average frame time, 99th percentile frame time, maximum frame time, GPU timing where available, CPU utilization, memory consumption, and generation times.

Prioritize consistent frame pacing and responsive gameplay rather than impressive but unstable peak FPS.

## 9. Autonomous development and creative freedom

You have complete freedom over the architecture, technology stack, programming languages, frameworks, engines, libraries, rendering techniques, and project structure.

There are absolutely no restrictions on the number of files. Do not force the project into one file.

Choose whatever approach produces the best combination of visual quality, performance, maintainability, and development efficiency.

Do not blindly reproduce the original architecture. Research, experiment, compare alternatives, and make independent engineering decisions.

Work autonomously. Do not wait for approval for ordinary technical or creative decisions.

## 10. Actual gameplay testing and visual verification

Testing is mandatory throughout development.

Actually launch the game and play it yourself. Do not rely exclusively on source code inspection, automated unit tests, or static screenshots.

Explore the island, enter buildings, climb staircases, operate doors, interact with objects, solve puzzles, follow quests, and complete the gameplay progression.

Generate multiple independent worlds using different seeds. Verify that procedural generation produces coherent and navigable environments every time.

Capture hundreds of screenshots and video sequences from different perspectives, locations, interiors, exteriors, weather conditions, lighting conditions, and gameplay situations.

Actually open and visually inspect the captured images and recordings.

Use a separate visual critic whenever possible. It should evaluate rendered images and videos without seeing the source code or implementation reasoning, so its feedback remains focused on the actual visual result.

Collect concrete criticism, prioritize defects, fix them, and repeat the process.

Test collision accuracy, interaction reliability, procedural architecture, visual consistency, frame pacing, and performance across different quality settings.

Create reproducible test scenarios and automated benchmarks where useful.

Do not declare the game complete simply because it launches or produces attractive screenshots.

## 11. GitHub development workflow

The official project repository is:

https://github.com/maxy618/the-last-beacon

Use this repository as the primary remote for the entire project.

Initialize the project in this repository and maintain its source code there throughout development.

You are expected to actively use Git and GitHub, not merely prepare the project locally.

- Commit changes regularly in meaningful, logically separated commits.
- Use clear and descriptive commit messages.
- Push working progress to the remote repository throughout development.
- Keep the main branch in a functional state.
- Create branches when useful for substantial independent changes.
- Do not overwrite remote history or use force pushes unnecessarily.
- Maintain a proper `.gitignore`. Do not commit dependencies, caches, temporary files, or unnecessarily large raw recordings.
- Keep useful development documentation, architecture notes, benchmark reports, and curated testing evidence in the repository.

Create GitHub Releases for substantial, verified playable milestones.

Use sensible semantic versioning and meaningful release notes. Distinguish experimental alpha builds from stable releases.

Attach the appropriate playable build and relevant documentation or performance reports to milestone releases whenever possible.

Eventually publish a final stable release, including the completed game, release notes, and verification results.

Before every release, verify that the corresponding commit is pushed and that the released build actually works.

If GitHub authentication or permissions prevent an operation, investigate the issue, continue local development, and clearly report the exact blocker. Never pretend that a commit, push, or release succeeded when it did not.

## 12. Final objective

Deliver **THE LAST BEACON** as a complete, polished, genuinely enjoyable procedural exploration game.

It must combine:

- Extraordinary graphical quality that surpasses Abyssal Garden.
- Sophisticated, believable procedural world and architecture generation.
- Unique, fully explorable buildings and lighthouses in every generated world.
- Accurate collision, physics, navigation, and interactions.
- A compelling, naturally written story with meaningful quests and subtle guidance.
- Engaging, polished first-person gameplay.
- Excellent performance and stable frame pacing.
- Thorough visual and gameplay testing.
- A properly maintained GitHub repository with meaningful commits, pushes, and releases.

Do not stop at a prototype, static environment, technical demonstration, or attractive opening scene.

Build a real game. Play it. Inspect it. Improve it. Benchmark it. Repeat.

The creative and technical direction is entirely yours. Pursue the highest possible quality without imposing artificial limitations on your implementation.
